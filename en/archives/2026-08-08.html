French Cinema Bounces Back

Published August 8, 2026

Share:

Scenarios The essentials Reference
The question

❓ After a historic drop in theater attendance in 2025 (-13.6%, the lowest level since 1997 outside the Covid years), will the spectacular rebound of early 2026 hold for the whole year, or is it just a blip before another decline?

Every year, the CNC* (the public body that tracks and funds French cinema) publishes its annual theater attendance report. And 2025 marked a shock: 156.79 million tickets sold for the year, down 13.6% from 2024 — 24 million fewer moviegoers in a single year, and the lowest level recorded since 1997, outside the Covid years.

The reason fits in one sentence from Gaëtan Bruel, president of the CNC: a "lackluster" year, short on crowd-pleasers* — the kind of films able to draw a very wide audience across every age group. In 2025, neither Hollywood nor French cinema produced enough of them: the market share* of French films fell back to 37.9% of admissions, while several highly anticipated American blockbusters disappointed at the box office.

Except 2026 is changing the picture entirely. Over the first seven months of the year, about 107.9 million tickets have already been sold in France, a pace well above 2025's. The first quarter grew 14%, and July jumped 22.6% year-on-year. Over the rolling twelve months* from August 2025 to July 2026, attendance reaches 174.52 million tickets, up from 167.56 million a year earlier — a real rebound driven in part by "Marsupilami," Philippe Lacheau's French comedy, which topped 6.1 million admissions and became one of the year's biggest French hits.

Late July delivered exactly the two highlights that explain much of that jump: The Odyssey, Christopher Nolan's sword-and-sandal epic starring Matt Damon, released July 15, posted the 3rd-best opening day of the year (232,972 admissions); then Spider-Man: Brand New Day, released July 29, logged the 9th-best opening week in French box office history with 3.24 million admissions in a single week on just 665 prints — the best superhero-movie opening since Avengers: Endgame in 2019.

These two hits aren't isolated cases: here, as of today (August 12, 2026), is the ranking of the ten highest-grossing films in France by admissions since the start of the year.

Still a moving ranking: The Odyssey, Spider-Man: Brand New Day and Toy Story 5, all three still in theaters, could still climb further — or even overtake one another — in the coming weeks.

The sector remains fragile, though. Overhead and rent for theaters keep rising, and streaming platforms — which now fund about 25% of French fiction, or €1.2 billion a year — are changing viewing habits, even as the media release-window rule* still largely shields theaters. The net balance of theater openings and closures stays nearly stable, with only 5 to 6 fewer screens than last year — a sign the crisis hasn't (yet) emptied theaters for good.

One big unknown remains: the fall season. Autumn is traditionally a quieter time for theaters, and the coming months' release calendar leans heavily on big American titles rather than new French hits.

🎬 The releases that will shape the fall season
  • 🗓️ The Dog Stars Ridley Scott, starring Jacob Elordi, Josh Brolin and Margaret Qualley — worldwide release August 26, 2026
  • 🗓️ Digger Alejandro González Iñárritu, starring Tom Cruise — worldwide release September 30, 2026

The first-half rebound also owes a lot to a comparison effect against a historically low 2025: the further the year goes, the more that effect fades. So no one can say today whether 2026 confirms a genuine turnaround or whether this surge will fade once summer ends — exactly the kind of situation where the numbers point in two directions depending on the period observed, which lends itself to the three-scenario exercise.

🎬 The rebound holds through year-end

In this scenario, the first-half momentum — admissions up 19.7% over the first seven months of 2026 — carries through to year-end. The fall and winter calendar lines up several big releases, French and American alike — including Avengers: Doomsday, the December 17 Marvel blockbuster whose advance sales are already twice as fast as Deadpool & Wolverine (2024), and Denis Villeneuve's Dune: Messiah the very next day — drawing large audiences without cannibalizing each other, on the model of summer 2026 where "Marsupilami" and "The Kingdom of Shadows" alone topped 10 million admissions. Total attendance finishes the year above 185 million, erasing most of 2025's drop and matching the 2023-2024 plateau (181 million).

This is more likely than the degraded scenario, because the rebound is already largely locked in over seven months and can no longer be fully erased; but less likely than the stable scenario, since it assumes fall — traditionally a quieter season — keeps pace with a summer carried by blockbusters, a riskier bet than a simple leveling-off.

🎟️ The recovery stalls halfway

This is the trajectory that best fits the numbers already in hand: much of the 2026 rebound is explained by a comparison effect against an exceptionally low 2025, and by the one-off success of a few big draws rather than a broad recovery across the board — French films' market share, at 37.9% in 2025, stays in the same range. The quieter fall season leans mainly on big American releases (the new Tom Cruise film, a Ridley Scott adaptation) rather than new French hits. The year finishes between 165 and 180 million admissions: better than 2025, but still below the 2023-2024 plateau of 181 million.

This is the most likely of the three scenarios: neither the favorable scenario's continued acceleration nor the degraded scenario's return to 2025's lows fits as well as a recovery that's already real but still fragile.

🍂 The rebound stalls this fall

In this scenario, the comparison effect inflating 2026's numbers fades as the year goes on — expected, since it's measured against an already-weak second half of 2025. Fall, dominated by big American releases rather than French ones, isn't enough to make up for it, and French films' market share slips below 35%, a new low. Total attendance finishes the year close to 2025's (156.79 million), confirming that last year's drop wasn't an isolated accident but the start of a deeper trend, fed by rising rent and overhead for exhibitors and by streaming's growing weight.

This is the least likely of the three scenarios: it would take a fall collapse far more severe than what industry professionals are forecasting today, given that seven months of rebound are already locked in.

The essentials

Will the rebound in French theater attendance, underway since early 2026, hold for the whole year? In 2025, attendance had hit its lowest level since 1997 (-13.6%); the first seven months of 2026 mark a real turnaround. Most likely answer (45%): the rebound holds at a steady pace through December, without necessarily accelerating further. Signal to watch: the performance of the big fall-winter releases (Avengers: Doomsday, Dune: Messiah, the new Tom Cruise film) — if they hit like Spider-Man and The Odyssey did this summer, the year could finish above 185 million admissions (30%); if fall stays quieter than usual, attendance will more likely settle between 165 and 180 million.

Quick glossary

CNC (National Centre for Cinema and the Moving Image)
The French public body that funds, regulates and publishes the official annual theater attendance statistics.
Crowd-pleasers
Films able to draw a very wide audience across every age group and background — the kind of hit that, on its own, drags overall theater attendance upward.
Market share (French films)
The share of theater admissions generated by films produced in France, compared to foreign (mainly American) films.
Rolling twelve months
A twelve-month measure that doesn't follow the calendar year (January-December) but moves forward month by month, useful for smoothing out seasonal dips and peaks.
Media release-window rule
The French rule that imposes a legal delay, of several months, between a film's theatrical release and its availability on streaming, to shield theaters from direct competition.
